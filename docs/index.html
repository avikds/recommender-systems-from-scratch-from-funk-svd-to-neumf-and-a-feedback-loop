Recommender Systems from Scratch: From Funk SVD to NeuMF and a Feedback Loop

How it was built

Part 1: A World With Known Answers

A seeded ratings world shaped like MovieLens-1M: planted low-rank tastes, user and item biases, popularity-skewed and preference-driven observations, 1-to-5 ratings at the book's 4.47 percent density, a per-user split, and summary statistics. Unlike real data, it tells you the true expected rating of every user-item pair.

Part 2: Baselines and Neighbours

The two rungs every recommender must beat: a global mean and regularized user and item biases. Then collaborative filtering in the sense of eq 22.1: similarities over co-rated entries and predictions built from a user's nearest neighbours, or an item's.

Part 3: Matrix Factorization

Section 22.1.3 in full: the zero-filled SVD demo of Figure 22.3 and why it fails, Funk's SGD updates (eq 22.6-22.9), alternating least squares, the PMF objective (eq 22.10) with lambda read off the priors, recovery of the planted factors, and the price of assuming that ratings are missing at random.

Part 4: AutoRec

The nonlinear version of matrix factorization from section 22.1.4: an autoencoder over rating vectors with a loss that counts observed entries only (eq 22.11-22.12), gradients by hand, Adam, and the item-based against user-based comparison.

Part 5: Implicit Feedback

Section 22.2: throw the rating values away and keep only who interacted with what. A leave-one-out protocol with ranking metrics, the preference triples of eq 22.15, Bayesian personalized ranking (eq 22.13-22.14), the hinge loss (eq 22.16), and a pointwise baseline with sampled negatives.

Part 6: Factorization Machines and Cold Start

Sections 22.2.2 and 22.3: one model family that treats users, items and side features alike. The design matrix of Figure 22.6, the O(KD) identity of eq 22.18-22.20, gradients and training, and a cold-start split where items in the test set have never been rated.

Part 7: Neural Matrix Factorization

Section 22.2.3 in tinygrad, where autograd earns its place: a GMF pathway and an MLP pathway with separate embeddings, fused into one logit (eq 22.22-22.24), trained on sampled negatives, and compared pathway by pathway against a plain dot product at the same budget.

Part 8: The Feedback Loop

Section 22.4: the training data is a consequence of earlier recommendations. A simulator where a system recommends, observes, retrains and repeats, comparing greedy, epsilon-greedy and Thompson-style policies on reward, regret, catalog coverage and what the model ends up knowing. Then one report that runs the whole project.
